faq:
- question: Why won't my cat use the scratching post I bought?
  answer: The most common reasons are that it's too short (cats want to fully stretch, not just reach), too wobbly to feel safe mid-scratch, or covered in the wrong texture — most cats prefer sisal rope or sisal fabric over carpet. Placement matters too; a post tucked in a spare room gets ignored compared to one near where your cat already scratches.
- question: Sisal rope or sisal fabric — does it matter?
  answer: Both work, but they wear differently. Sisal rope (wound tightly around a post) tends to hold up longer under heavy use and shows wear more gradually, while sisal fabric can shred and look tatty sooner, though it's often cheaper to replace or is sold as a swappable pad.
- question: How tall does a scratching post need to be?
  answer: As a rule of thumb, at least 70-80cm so an adult cat can fully extend up on their hind legs. Shorter posts around 40-45cm can still get used, especially by kittens or in tight spaces, but a full stretch is part of what makes scratching satisfying for a cat, so taller is generally better if you have the room.
- question: My cat scratches the sofa even though we have a post — what now?
  answer: Try moving the post right next to the spot they're currently scratching, then gradually shift it to where you'd actually like it over a few weeks. Covering the sofa corner temporarily with foil or a throw, combined with a nearby post, tends to redirect the habit faster than the post alone.

A scratching post that wobbles, is too short to stretch on, or falls apart within weeks just pushes your cat back towards the sofa. Here's what actually holds up, and which posts and trees are worth buying.

## What actually matters

- **Genuine height for a full stretch** — look for at least 70-80cm on a standalone post; anything shorter suits kittens or tight corners but won't satisfy an adult cat's full stretch.
- **A stable, weighted base** — a post that rocks or tips over on the first scratch teaches a cat to avoid it, often permanently.
- **Sisal rope over cheap carpet** — tightly wound sisal rope holds up to repeated scratching far longer than carpet or thin fabric wrapping, which shreds and looks tatty within weeks.
- **Placement near where your cat already scratches** — even the best post gets ignored if it's tucked away in a room your cat doesn't spend time in.
