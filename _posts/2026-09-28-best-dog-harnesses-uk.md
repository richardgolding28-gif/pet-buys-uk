- question: Harness or collar for daily walks?
  answer: Harness, for most dogs. It spreads pressure across the chest instead of the neck, which matters a lot for breeds prone to tracheal issues and for any dog that pulls. Collars are still fine for holding ID tags.
- question: How do I know if a harness actually fits?
  answer: You should be able to fit two fingers flat under any strap, but no more than that. Check it after a few minutes of walking too, since some harnesses loosen as the dog moves.
- question: What does "no-pull" actually mean?
  answer: The lead clips to a ring on the front of the chest instead of the back. When the dog pulls forward, it gently turns them back toward you rather than letting them lean into the pull the way a back-clip harness allows.

A harness that doesn't fit properly either lets an anxious dog slip free, or rubs them raw under the front legs within a few walks. Here's what to check before buying, and which ones consistently fit well.

## What actually matters

- **Adjustable at multiple points**, not just one strap — chest and neck girth both need independent adjustment for a proper fit.
- **A front clip ring** if pulling is an issue — this is what makes a harness "no-pull" rather than just a harness.
- **Padded straps** where the harness crosses the chest and belly, especially for daily walkers.
- **Reflective stitching or panels** if you ever walk in low light — genuinely useful, not just a marketing add-on.
