- question: Can I use a dog flea treatment on my cat, or vice versa?
  answer: No — never use a dog-specific spot-on on a cat. Many dog formulas contain permethrin, which is highly toxic to cats even in small amounts. Always buy a product labelled for the species you're treating, and double-check if you have both a cat and dog that groom each other.
- question: Do I need to treat the house as well as the dog?
  answer: If you already have a flea problem rather than just preventing one, yes. Adult fleas on your dog are a small fraction of the total infestation — eggs, larvae and pupae in carpets, bedding and soft furnishings make up the rest, so treating the dog alone without washing bedding and vacuuming thoroughly tends to see fleas bounce back within weeks.

For most UK owners, a reliable monthly spot-on or an 8-month collar covers it without overcomplicating things.

A flea infestation is far easier to prevent than to get rid of once it's taken hold in your home, and ticks carry genuine disease risk in the UK's grassier areas. Here's what actually matters when choosing treatment, and which products are worth buying.

## What actually matters

- **Confirm it covers both fleas and ticks** — some budget products only target fleas, which matters if your dog walks through woodland, long grass, or deer-heavy areas where tick exposure is real.
- **Match the dose to your dog's actual weight** — spot-on treatments are sold in weight bands (small/medium/large/XL), and under-dosing a dog at the top of a band is a common reason treatment appears not to work.
- **Check the reapplication window** — most spot-ons need repeating every 4 weeks; missing that window is the single biggest reason owners think a product has "stopped working."
- **Look at active ingredient overlap if you're also worming** — some flea/tick products are combined with wormers, which can be convenient but also means checking you're not accidentally double-dosing the same ingredient from two different products.
- **Factor in the household, not just the dog** — a multi-cat or multi-dog home needs every animal treated at once, since fleas move freely between pets that share bedding or furniture.
