- question: Enclosed or open tray?
  answer: Enclosed generally controls odour and litter tracking better, but some cats genuinely refuse to use a covered tray. If you're switching an existing cat, introduce it alongside the old tray first rather than replacing it outright.
- question: How often does litter actually need changing?
  answer: Scoop solids daily. For clumping litter, a full change every 2-3 weeks is typical for one cat; for non-clumping, more like weekly. Multiple cats sharing a tray need more frequent full changes regardless of litter type.
- question: One tray per cat, or can they share?
  answer: The general guidance is one tray per cat plus one extra, since some cats simply won't share regardless of how clean it's kept. Worth trying if you're seeing accidents outside the tray with multiple cats in the house.

A litter tray that's too shallow, too small, or impossible to clean properly ends up being the reason for accidents elsewhere in the house. Here's what actually matters, and which trays hold up.

## What actually matters

- **High sides or a lid** — controls both litter kicked outside the tray and the smell, though see the FAQ below on whether your specific cat will tolerate a cover.
- **A wide, genuinely easy-clean opening** — a small flip-top that makes scooping awkward gets used less often, which defeats the point.
- **Non-stick surface** — stainless steel and treated plastic both resist litter and waste sticking, unlike basic untreated plastic which stains and holds odour over time.
- **Size relative to your cat** — the tray should be at least 1.5 times your cat's body length; too small is one of the most common reasons cats avoid using a tray at all.
